# Fable Narrative Language Firewall

Use this reference for the title and narrative paragraphs of every new output.
The factual debrief uses real technical language. Historical Bridge and
Everyday behavior is compatibility documentation, not an authoring route.

## Three isolated surfaces

Keep three surfaces separate from the first outline onward:

1. **Private technical preflight:** retain the real terms, symbols, equations,
   metric identities, evidence levels, source conditions, and unresolved links.
2. **Sealed narrative:** express one supported causal relation entirely through
   objects, actions, quantities, and observable consequences native to one
   familiar story world.
3. **Factual debrief:** after the story ends, restore the canonical name,
   technical vocabulary, formal rule, exact metrics, evidence level, scope, and
   limits in the existing `1 -> 2 -> 3 -> 4` order.

Technical precision on the first surface does not authorize technical language
on the second. The narrative preserves the causal structure; the debrief
restores the professional representation.

Hiding terminology must not erase the source's task, constraints or reason for
the selected relation. Apply `story-output-contract.md`'s source-context binding
check through in-world goals and choices, not paper/reviewer voice. A familiar,
reusable scene is valid when its necessary source constraints remain recoverable.

## Build a private denylist and story-world allowlist

Before drafting, derive a case-specific **narrative denylist** from the selected
source and planned debrief. Include:

- the canonical concept name and every alias;
- domain-specific objects, methods, devices, operations, and mechanism labels;
- variables, standard abbreviations, equations, set notation, formal units, and
  statistical notation;
- formal metric names and their paper-specific labels; and
- source or reviewer voice such as references to a paper, author, reported
  experiment, proof status, unsupported generalization, or cross-system
  guarantee.

Do not serialize this denylist or add it to the daily handoff. It is a private
authoring gate, not a schema field or a universal static dictionary.

Also write a compact **story-world allowlist** containing the setting's ordinary
objects, visible actions, causal rules, quantities, and connective language.
Every mechanism-bearing narrative sentence must be expressible through that
allowlist. Ordinary background nouns need not be enumerated, but a new noun or
verb that carries a technical mapping must be added deliberately and audited.

If a necessary technical relation has no faithful story-world expression,
narrow the relation and try one repair. If a standalone repair still requires domain terminology, report the obstacle
without switching modes. A daily opening
uses the ranked-source fallback in `daily-briefing-interface.md` instead of
publishing a contaminated fable.

## Keep the story world closed

From the story title through its final narrative paragraph:

- every sentence describes an in-world state, goal, rule, action, choice,
  observation, or consequence;
- no sentence names the source, author, paper, study, evidence class, proof
  status, calibration scope, or generalization limit;
- no domain term, method name, device name, formal metric label, variable,
  formula, acronym, unit, or set notation appears;
- symbols must be rendered as ordinary story actions and quantities rather than
  as a second notation system; and
- the canonical concept name is revealed only after the narrative ends, in
  factual section 1, for both standalone and daily Fable Mode.

Evidence uncertainty still constrains the story. Represent it as an observable
in-world limit: the actor may see the operation and outcome but lack access to
the hidden intermediate cause. Put the literal statement about what the source
does or does not establish in the debrief. Never continue the narrative by
inventing an intermediate mechanism.

The governing-rule origin also remains visible, but in story-world language.
Explain why the tracked quantity can change, why each in-world input matters,
and what rule permits the action before using it. Reserve the formal derivation,
protocol name, source attribution, and evidence classification for the debrief.

## Project quantitative evidence into story language

Use a number in the narrative only when it helps the reader understand the
selected mechanism. Do not place a formal metric name, percent sign, error bar,
unit, denominator label, or statistical qualifier in the sealed narrative.

When a rate matters, translate it into an in-world frequency:

- use an exact integer ratio when it remains simple;
- otherwise use an explicitly approximate normalized frequency over a familiar
  group;
- phrase a normalized group as an explanatory projection, not as the source's
  literal sample size; and
- omit the number from the story when the mechanism is clear without it.

The debrief or worked example restores the exact source value, metric identity,
numerator, denominator, conditioning set, population or device, time or round
scope, and reported uncertainty. The story and formal block therefore share
the same **underlying data identity**, direction, conditions, comparison, and
conclusion; they do not need the same surface notation. A rounded story count
must be visibly approximate and must never silently replace the source value.

## Restore terminology only after the boundary

After the final story paragraph:

- section 1 reveals the canonical name and a scope-bounded definition;
- section 2 maps every mechanism-bearing story action to the real operation,
  rule source, evidence level, changed/preserved/lost content, observable
  result, and unsupported stronger conclusion;
- sections 3 and 4 state evidence conditions, residual risks, analogy limits,
  and tempting false inferences; and
- the worked example may use formal terms and notation only after section 1 or
  2 has introduced and mapped them. A new term in the example first receives a
  plain-language clause before its formal label.

The debrief must restore every professional distinction hidden from the story.
Closed-world prose is not permission to merge operations, weaken conditions,
change a metric, or omit uncertainty.

## Fail-closed audit

Audit the finished title and every complete narrative paragraph, not a prefix:

1. **Lexicon audit:** reject any denylisted term, formal symbol, metric label,
   unit, or source/reviewer phrase.
2. **Perspective audit:** move any sentence that comments on evidence, authors,
   proof, scope, or generalization into the debrief.
3. **Causal round trip:** replace story objects and actions with the mapping and
   verify that the supported technical mechanism can be reconstructed without
   an invented step.
4. **Data-identity audit:** verify that every story frequency projects the same
   source datum and conditions restored in the worked example; mark rounding.
5. **Restoration audit:** verify that sections 1 and 2 restore every technical
   distinction intentionally hidden from the narrative.

Failure of any audit requires a rewrite, a narrower relation, or the defined
source fallback or explicit failure. Do not repair a contaminated narrative by adding more
technical explanation inside it.
